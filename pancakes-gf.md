# Pancakes — Gluten-Free & Nut-Free, Optional Banana (King Arthur Measure for Measure)

Target: **tall, tender, fluffy diner-style pancakes** with no gummy center and no gritty/rice-y texture, built on King Arthur Gluten-Free Measure for Measure flour. One base batter, with a **banana option** you pick at the mixing stage (mashed into the batter, sliced onto it, or both).

The base is King Arthur's own **Simply Perfect Pancakes**, their most popular pancake recipe. King Arthur states that making it with Measure for Measure swapped 1:1 for all-purpose gives "the same result." On top of that base, this version adds the techniques the most popular gluten-free pancake recipes agree on: a mandatory batter rest, gentler heat for the banana version, and weighing the flour.

---

## Why this works (the actual mechanism)

- **Measure for Measure already contains xanthan gum**, which acts as the binder. **Don't add more.** Extra xanthan is the #1 cause of gummy, rubbery gluten-free pancakes. (King Arthur's *Gluten-Free All-Purpose* flour is a different product with no gum, which is why their separate GF pancake recipe adds xanthan. Don't mix up the two bags.)
- **The 15-minute rest is not optional here.** Rice and sorghum flours and tapioca and potato starches hydrate much more slowly than wheat flour. Unrested gluten-free batter cooks up gritty, then gummy. Rested batter thickens noticeably, turns smooth, and holds its bubbles. Every top recipe calls for a rest of 5–15 min, and King Arthur's GF recipe specifically calls for 15.
- **Foamy eggs = lift.** Beating the eggs and milk for about 3 minutes before adding anything else whips air into the batter. With no gluten to trap gas, that extra air noticeably improves height. This step comes straight from King Arthur's Simply Perfect method.
- **Banana changes the batter, so the recipe adjusts for it.** Mashed banana is about 75% water, dense, and full of sugar. Stirred in with no other changes, it gives you thin batter, pancakes that burn outside and stay raw and gummy inside, and a flat stack. So the banana version **cuts the milk and sugar**, **adds a pinch of baking soda** (banana is mildly acidic, and the soda neutralizes it for extra lift, following Well Fed Baker's 1:1 banana ratio), and **cooks at a lower temperature** so the center sets before the outside over-browns.

## Nut-free notes (read these, since GF products often share facilities with nuts)

- **King Arthur Measure for Measure** has no nut ingredients (rice, brown rice, sorghum, tapioca, potato starch, cellulose, xanthan). **Still check the allergen statement on your bag** before each purchase, because facility and labeling claims can change between production runs.
- **Milk:** use dairy milk or, if you need dairy-free, **oat milk (certified GF) or soy milk**. **Not almond, cashew, or coconut-almond blends.** Many "dairy-free pancake" recipes default to almond milk.
- **Butter/oil:** butter, or a neutral oil such as canola or avocado. Avoid "baking spray" blends that list nut oils, and check any plant-based butter for cashew or almond.
- **Vanilla:** pure vanilla extract is fine. Avoid "vanilla-almond" or imitation blends that carry nut warnings.
- **Toppings are where nuts usually sneak in:** granola, nut butters, praline syrups, and bakery chocolate chips (look for a nut-free brand such as Enjoy Life).

- Fine sea salt
- Organic pure vanilla extract

**Produce (banana version)**
- 1–2 **very ripe** bananas (heavily brown-speckled). Under-ripe bananas bring starch instead of sugar and make pancakes gummier.

## Equipment
- Kitchen scale (**use it**, because gluten-free flour packs unpredictably in a cup; 180 g is roughly 1½ cups spooned and leveled)
- Two bowls + whisk (or a hand mixer for the egg foam)
- Electric griddle, or a heavy skillet/cast iron
- ¼-cup measure or #16 scoop for portioning
- Thin, wide spatula
- Instant-read thermometer (optional, but it's the easiest way to rule out a gummy center)

---

## Before You Start
- **Eggs (2 large, about 100 g) and milk: room temp, or microwave the milk to about 100°F (lukewarm).** Cold milk hitting melted butter seizes the butter into little flecks, and cold batter takes longer to hydrate during the rest.
- **Butter (43 g, 3 Tbsp):** melt and let cool a couple of minutes so it's warm, not hot.
- **Banana version:** mash the banana with a fork until **mostly smooth**. A few small lumps are fine, but large chunks create wet, gummy pockets. Measure it by weight.
- **Heat the griddle during the batter rest** so both are ready at the same time.

## Ingredients — Base Batch (~10 four-inch pancakes, serves 3–4)

| | **Plain** | **Banana (mashed in)** |
|---|---|---|
| King Arthur GF Measure for Measure | 180 g (~1½ cups) | 180 g (~1½ cups) |
| Baking powder | 2 tsp | 2 tsp |
| Baking soda | — | ¼ tsp |
| Fine sea salt | ¾ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Cane sugar | 25 g (2 Tbsp) | 12 g (1 Tbsp) |
| Cinnamon | — | ½ tsp (optional) |
| Large eggs | 2 (about 100 g) | 2 (about 100 g) |
| Milk | 283 g (1¼ cups) | 227 g (1 cup) |
| Very ripe banana, mashed | — | 115 g (~½ cup, about 1 large) |
| Melted butter (or neutral oil) | 43 g (3 Tbsp) | 43 g (3 Tbsp) |
| Vanilla extract | 1 tsp | 1 tsp |
| **Griddle temp** | **375°F** | **350°F** |

> **Sliced-banana option (works with either batter):** thinly slice 1 banana, about ⅛". After pouring each pancake, lay 4–5 slices on the wet top, then flip as usual. This follows King Arthur's own blueberry tip of adding fruit to the surface rather than mixing it in. You get caramelized banana coins without changing the batter's hydration. For maximum banana, combine this with the mashed-in batter.

## Method

### 1) Dry mix (1 min)
Whisk the Measure for Measure, baking powder, salt, and sugar together, plus the baking soda and cinnamon if you're making the banana version. Set aside.

### 2) Foam the eggs (3 min)
Beat the eggs and lukewarm milk together for about **3 minutes**, until foamy and light. A hand mixer on medium makes this easy. This is your main lift source, so don't skip it.

### 3) Finish the wet (30 sec)
Whisk in the melted butter and vanilla, and the mashed banana if using. Stir it in gently so you don't knock out all the foam.

### 4) Combine (20–30 sec)
Pour the wet into the dry and **fold gently** just until no dry flour remains. A few small lumps are fine. Gluten-free batter can't get "tough" from overmixing the way wheat batter can, but heavy mixing deflates the egg foam from step 2.

### 5) Rest (15 min, not optional)
Leave the batter at room temp for **15 minutes** while the griddle heats. It will visibly thicken and get bubbly. **After the rest**, if it's too thick to spread slowly on its own (it should pour like thick, slow lava, not sit in a mound), stir in milk **1 Tbsp at a time**. King Arthur's rule: more milk = thinner pancakes, less = thicker and fluffier.

### 6) Cook
- Griddle at **375°F plain / 350°F banana**, or medium (plain) / medium-low (banana) on a stovetop. **Test it first:** a drop of water should skitter and evaporate immediately. Then cook **one small test pancake** and adjust the heat before committing the whole batch.
- Lightly butter or oil the surface. Wipe it between batches so browned butter doesn't scorch.
- Pour **¼ cup** per pancake. **Don't spread it or press it down.**
- **Flip once**, when bubbles cover the surface, the edges look dry and matte, and the underside is golden. That's about **2 min plain, 3–4 min banana**. Cook the second side **1½–2 min**.
- **Doneness check:** GF pancakes can look done outside while the center stays gummy, especially the banana version. Probe the center of the first pancake: **200–205°F** means it's set. Or tear it open: the crumb should be fluffy and springy, not wet or glossy.

### 7) Hold warm
Keep finished pancakes in a single layer on a rack in a **200°F oven**. Stacking them traps steam, which turns GF pancakes gummy fast.

## Troubleshooting checklist
- **Gummy / wet center** → usually heat that's too high (outside finishes before inside), or the batter wasn't rested. Drop the heat 25°F and extend the first side by a minute. Banana version: confirm you cut the milk to 227 g, and use a scale on the banana. Also double-check you didn't add xanthan gum on top of Measure for Measure.
- **Gritty / sandy** → under-rested. Give it the full 15 min, or even 20.
- **Flat / dense** → batter too thin (use less milk next time), eggs not foamed long enough, stale baking powder (test: ½ tsp in hot water should fizz hard), or the batter sat more than about 45 min and the leavening faded.
- **Burnt outside, pale inside (banana)** → banana sugars caramelize fast. Keep it at 350°F or even 340°F, and don't make pancakes bigger than ¼ cup.
- **Pale and rubbery** → griddle too cool. Bump the heat 15–25°F.
- **Too thick to spread** → normal after the rest. Loosen with milk 1 Tbsp at a time.

---

## Scaling & make-ahead
- **Double batch:** multiply everything by 2 (360 g flour, 4 eggs, etc.). The rest time stays at 15 min.
- **Waffles:** the plain batter works in a waffle iron. Add an extra 28 g (2 Tbsp) melted butter for crispness (King Arthur's GF waffle tip).
- **Freezing:** cool the cooked pancakes completely on a rack, freeze them in a single layer, then bag them. Reheat in a toaster or a 350°F oven. Don't microwave them, which makes GF pancakes rubbery.
- **Batter doesn't hold well overnight:** the baking powder fades and GF starches keep thickening. Mix it fresh.
