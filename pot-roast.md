# Midwest Sunday Pot Roast — Dutch Oven • 4 servings

Classic one-pot Midwest pot roast: a seared 2 lb chuck roast braised low and slow in beef broth with onion, then carrots, celery, and potatoes added partway through so they finish tender but not mushy. The braising liquid becomes the gravy.

**Potatoes: cooked in the pot, not mashed.** That's the Midwest Sunday-dinner way — the potatoes soak up the beef juices and it's all one pot. Yukon Golds hold their shape in a braise better than russets. If you'd rather have mashed potatoes, see [Variations](#variations).

**Total time:** ~3½ hours (30 min hands-on). **Oven:** 300°F.

## Ingredients

### Roast
- 2 lb beef chuck roast
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 2 Tbsp neutral oil

### Braise
- 1 large yellow onion, cut into 8 wedges (root end left on so they hold together)
- 4 garlic cloves, smashed and peeled
- 1 Tbsp tomato paste
- 2 cups low-sodium beef broth
- 1 Tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme (or 1 tsp dried)
- 2 bay leaves

### Vegetables (go in partway through)
- 1 lb carrots (about 5), peeled, cut into 2-inch chunks
- 3 celery stalks, cut into 2-inch pieces
- 1½ lb Yukon Gold potatoes, halved or quartered into ~2-inch pieces (no need to peel)
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ½ cup low-sodium beef broth (extra, only if the liquid has cooked down)

### Gravy
- 2 Tbsp all-purpose flour
- ¼ cup cold water
- Salt & pepper to taste

### Finish
- Chopped fresh parsley (optional)

## Before You Start

- **No need to let the roast come to room temp.** Searing straight from the fridge is fine for a 3-hour braise — the long cook evens everything out, and a cold surface actually helps you get a deep crust before the inside overcooks. The most important thing is that the meat is **dry** on the surface.
- **Gas oven notes:** Preheat a full 20 minutes so the walls are hot and the temperature stabilizes. Use the **middle rack** — gas ovens heat from the bottom, and the lowest rack can scorch the bottom of the pot. If your oven runs hot (an oven thermometer will tell you), set it to 285°F.
- **Prep the vegetables while the roast braises** — you have 2 hours of downtime, so there's no rush up front.

---

## Method

### 1) Preheat and season (5 min)
Move a rack to the middle of the oven and preheat to **300°F**. Pat the chuck roast (2 lb) very dry with paper towels on all sides. Season all over with kosher salt (1½ tsp) and black pepper (1 tsp), pressing it in.

### 2) Sear the roast (10–12 min)
Set the Dutch oven over **medium-high** heat and add the neutral oil (2 Tbsp). When the oil shimmers, lay in the roast and **don't move it** for 4–5 minutes, until a deep brown crust forms. Flip and sear the other side 4–5 minutes, then use tongs to stand it on its edges for about 30 seconds each. Move the roast to a plate.

*Why:* this browning is where most of the "pot roast" flavor comes from. Pale gray meat = bland braise. If the oil starts smoking hard, drop to medium.

### 3) Onion, garlic, tomato paste (5 min)
Lower the heat to **medium**. Add the onion (1 large, in wedges) cut-side down to the fat in the pot and cook 3 minutes until they pick up some color. Add the garlic (4 cloves) and tomato paste (1 Tbsp), stirring the paste into the fat for about 1 minute until it darkens a shade.

### 4) Deglaze and build the braise (3 min)
Pour in the beef broth (2 cups) and Worcestershire sauce (1 Tbsp). Scrape the bottom hard with a wooden spoon to lift all the browned bits — that's your gravy flavor. Add the thyme (4 sprigs, or 1 tsp dried) and bay leaves (2). Nestle the roast back in along with any juices from the plate. The liquid should come about **one-third to halfway up** the side of the roast; if not, add a splash more broth.

Bring it to a simmer on the stovetop.

### 5) First braise (2 hours)
Cover with the lid and move the pot to the middle rack of the 300°F oven. Braise **2 hours**. Leave it alone — no peeking needed.

Meanwhile, cut the carrots (1 lb), celery (3 stalks), and potatoes (1½ lb) into ~2-inch chunks. Keep the potatoes in a bowl of cold water so they don't brown.

### 6) Add the vegetables (5 min)
Pull the pot out and set it on the stovetop. Flip the roast. Drain the potatoes and arrange the carrots (1 lb), celery (3 stalks), and potatoes (1½ lb) around the roast, pushing them down into the liquid as much as possible. Sprinkle the vegetables with kosher salt (½ tsp). If the liquid has dropped below about 1 inch deep, add the extra beef broth (½ cup).

### 7) Second braise (1 to 1¼ hours)
Cover and return to the oven for **1 hour**, then check:
- **Roast:** a fork should slide in and twist with almost no resistance; it should start pulling apart. (Internal temp ~200–205°F if you're checking.) If it fights back, it isn't done — chuck gets *tougher* before it gets tender. Give it 15–20 more minutes and check again.
- **Potatoes and carrots:** a knife slides through easily.

### 8) Rest (10 min)
Move the roast to a cutting board and tent loosely with foil. Use a slotted spoon to move the vegetables to a serving platter and tent those too. Fish out and discard the bay leaves (2) and thyme stems.

### 9) Make the gravy (5–7 min)
Tilt the pot and spoon off most of the fat floating on top (leave a tablespoon or so for flavor). You should have about 1½–2 cups of liquid; if you have less, top it up with broth.

In a small jar, shake or whisk the flour (2 Tbsp) with the cold water (¼ cup) until no lumps remain. Bring the liquid in the pot to a simmer over **medium** heat and whisk in the slurry. Simmer 3–5 minutes, whisking, until it thickens enough to coat a spoon and no longer tastes floury. Taste and season with salt and pepper.

### 10) Serve
Slice the roast against the grain into thick slices, or just pull it into big chunks with two forks. Arrange with the vegetables, pour gravy over everything, and sprinkle with parsley if using. Pass extra gravy at the table.

## Doneness and troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Meat is tough or springy | Not cooked long enough | Back in the oven, covered, 20–30 min at a time |
| Meat is dry and stringy | Liquid cooked away, or oven too hot | Check oven temp with a thermometer; keep liquid 1/3 up the roast; make sure lid fits snugly |
| Potatoes are mushy | Added too early or cut too small | Keep them at 2-inch chunks and add at the 2-hour mark |
| Gravy is thin | Not enough reduction or flour | Simmer longer, or add another 1 Tbsp flour + 2 Tbsp cold water slurry |
| Gravy is too salty | Regular (not low-sodium) broth reduced down | Thin with water, add a few potato chunks to simmer in it |

## Variations

- **Mashed potatoes instead:** Leave the potatoes out of the pot (add an extra ½ lb carrots instead). Boil 2 lb peeled Yukon Golds in salted water 15–20 min until tender, drain, and mash with 4 Tbsp butter, ½ cup warm milk, and salt. Serve the roast and gravy over the top.
- **Onion soup mix (old-school Midwest):** Swap the 1½ tsp salt on the roast for 1 packet (1 oz) dry onion soup mix stirred into the broth. Skip the salt on the vegetables too — the mix is salty.
- **Red wine:** Replace ½ cup of the beef broth with dry red wine in step 4 for a deeper, richer gravy.
- **Leftovers:** Shred the leftover beef with gravy for hot beef sandwiches on white bread with mashed potatoes — a Midwest diner classic.
