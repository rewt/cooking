# Monster Cookies — Gluten-Free, Sunflower Butter (v1 Test Batch)

Our own scratch version of [Miss Jones Baking Co. Monster Cookie Mix](https://missjones.co/products/everyday-delicious-monster-cookie-mix), built around sunflower seed butter. The targets are **a slightly bigger cookie, a chewy crumb that holds together, and a lighter sunflower taste**.

> **Status: not baked yet.** The amounts come from the mix's label and published recipes, not from a tested batch. Bake this as a test, then adjust it with the tasting checklist.

---

## The bag vs. this version

The mix contains gluten-free oats, brown sugar, chocolate candies, chocolate chips, baking soda, and sea salt, with no flour or starch. You add 1 egg, ½ cup peanut butter, and 2 Tbsp softened butter, then bake at 350°F.

The label doesn't give amounts for the mix, so the **bag column is an estimate**. It's worked back from the nutrition panel (18 cookies, about 144 g sugar, 45 g fat, and 1,530 mg sodium in total) and the ingredient order.

|---|---|---|
| Oats | about 105–120 g | 140 g, with 40 g ground into flour |
| Sunflower butter | 128 g (½ cup) | 115 g |
| Butter | 28 g | 28 g |
| Egg | 1 | 1 + 1 yolk |

| Yield | 18 at about 28 g | 17 at about 35 g |
| Sunflower butter share of dough | about 25% | about 19% |

## What changed, and why (the actual mechanism)

Heavy and crumbly share a cause here. A flourless oat cookie is mostly oats held together by egg and seed butter, and seed butter is about half fat. Fat coats the oats, which slows hydration and keeps them from sticking to each other. So the main move is to use less sunflower butter and give the binding job to things that aren't fat.

- **About 23% less sunflower butter per bite.** Each bigger cookie has about the same sunflower butter as one bag-size cookie (about 7 g), spread through 25% more dough.
- **Some of the oats are ground.** Oat flour fills the gaps between whole flakes and thickens as it hydrates, giving the flakes something to stick to.
- **A 30-minute rest.** The bag's dough goes straight into the oven, and its directions warn that it may still be crumbly. Resting gives the oats time to absorb moisture before baking.
- **One extra yolk.** Yolks add chew and emulsify the fat, so the sunflower oil stays in the dough. It adds only about 5 g of fat to the whole batch.
- **A little honey.** It holds moisture and works as a glue, the same way syrup binds a granola bar. It also keeps the cookie pliable after it cools.
- **Much less baking soda.** The bag's cookies puff and then flatten as they cool, which likely leaves a more open, fragile crumb. Baking soda can also turn sunflower butter green, so this version uses less of it and adds some baking powder for lift.
- **Vanilla and a bit more salt.** Both lift the earthy sunflower flavor without adding any weight.
- **Mini chocolate only.** Large pieces act as fault lines where a low-structure cookie cracks.
- **A 1½ Tbsp scoop (about 35 g).** That's 25% bigger than the bag's regular cookie and smaller than its giant one (about 42 g).

## Nut-free notes

This recipe has no nut ingredients, but the finished cookie is only nut-free if the labels agree:

- **Candies:** standard M&M's carry a peanut "may contain" warning. If you need these nut-free, choose a candy labeled that way.
- **Chocolate chips:** Enjoy Life makes mini chips free from peanuts and tree nuts.
- **Sunflower butter:** some brands share equipment with peanuts or tree nuts, so check the jar.

## Before You Start
- **Butter (28 g / 2 Tbsp):** softened, so a finger leaves a dent. Not melted.
- **Egg + egg yolk (1 large egg, about 50 g, + 1 yolk, about 18 g):** room temp, so the butter doesn't firm up when they go in. Save the extra white for something else.
- **Sunflower butter (115 g / about 7 Tbsp):** room temp and **stirred well before weighing**. If the oil has separated, the top of the jar makes greasy dough and the bottom makes dry dough.
- **Salt (⅜ tsp):** check the sunflower butter's ingredients. If they include salt, use ¼ tsp.
- **Everything else** is temperature-neutral.

### If ingredients can't come to room temp
Straight-from-the-fridge is fine. Use these quick fixes, or mix everything cold and adjust.

**Quick warm-ups (5–10 min total):**
- **Egg + yolk (1 large egg, about 50 g, + 1 yolk, about 18 g):** set both whole eggs in a bowl of warm tap water for **5–10 minutes**, then crack one and separate the other.
- **Butter (28 g / 2 Tbsp):** cut into ½" cubes and leave on the counter for about 10 minutes, or microwave in **3–5 second bursts** until a finger leaves a dent. A little melting won't hurt at this small amount.
- **Sunflower butter (115 g / about 7 Tbsp):** if the jar was in the fridge, weigh it into a bowl and microwave in **15-second bursts**, stirring between, until it's soft and spoonable but not hot.

**Cooking with everything cold (no time to warm anything):**
- **Swap the butter for neutral oil (21 g / 1½ Tbsp).** Cold butter won't blend into stiff sunflower butter, and the lumps melt out in the oven. Oil stays liquid and blends right in, though you lose a little butter flavor.
- **Beat the wet ingredients 1–2 minutes longer.** Cold sunflower butter and cold eggs take longer to combine, and any streaks bake into weak spots.
- **Rest the dough 45 minutes instead of 30, on the counter.** Oats hydrate more slowly in cold dough, and this rest does much of the anti-crumble work.
- **Bake 1–2 minutes longer, still at 350°F.** Heat takes longer to reach the center of cold dough. Judge doneness by the center, because the edges will get ahead of it.

## Ingredients — Test Batch (~17 cookies)

### Dry
- 100 g certified gluten-free old-fashioned rolled oats (1¼ cups), left whole
- 40 g certified gluten-free old-fashioned rolled oats (½ cup), ground in step 1
- ½ tsp baking powder
- ¼ tsp baking soda
- ⅜ tsp fine sea salt (¼ tsp if your sunflower butter contains salt)

### Wet
- 28 g organic unsalted butter (2 Tbsp), softened
- 115 g sunflower seed butter (about 7 Tbsp), stirred well
- 85 g organic light brown sugar (about 6 Tbsp, packed)
- 21 g honey (1 Tbsp)
- 1 large organic egg (about 50 g) + 1 organic egg yolk (about 18 g), room temp
- 1½ tsp organic vanilla extract

### Mix-in
- 70 g mini candy-coated chocolates
- 55 g mini chocolate chips

---

## Method

### 1) Grind the oats (1 min)
Blend the oats for grinding (40 g / ½ cup) into a coarse flour, mostly powder with a few small flakes left.

### 2) Dry mix (1 min)
Whisk the ground oats (40 g), whole oats (100 g / 1¼ cups), baking powder (½ tsp), baking soda (¼ tsp), and salt (⅜ tsp, or ¼ tsp if your sunflower butter contains salt) together. Set aside.

### 3) Beat the butters and sweeteners (1–2 min)
In a large bowl, beat the butter (28 g / 2 Tbsp), sunflower butter (115 g / about 7 Tbsp), brown sugar (85 g), and honey (21 g / 1 Tbsp) on medium-low until smooth. Stop there, because whipping in air makes a cakier, more fragile cookie.

### 4) Egg + yolk + vanilla (30–45 sec)
Add the egg (1 large, about 50 g), egg yolk (1, about 18 g), and vanilla (1½ tsp). Mix on medium-low until fully combined with no streaks.

### 5) Add the dry mix and chocolate (1 min)
Add the dry mix from step 2 (140 g oats plus the leaveners and salt) and mix on low until no dry oats remain. Fold in the candies (70 g) and chocolate chips (55 g) with a spatula.

### 6) Rest (30 min, not optional)
Cover the bowl and leave it on the counter for **30 minutes**. The dough will firm up as the oats absorb moisture, which is the point. If you want to bake later, it can stay covered in the fridge for up to 24 hours. Add 1–2 minutes to the bake if the dough is still cold.

About 15 minutes into the rest, heat the oven to **350°F (177°C)** with a rack in the middle, and line the baking sheets with parchment.

If the dough falls apart instead of clumping after the rest, mix in milk or water (1 tsp at a time, up to 1 Tbsp).

### 7) Scoop and shape (3–5 min)
Scoop into ~35 g mounds with the 1½ Tbsp / #40 scoop, about 17 total. Weigh the first few to check the size. Squeeze each mound into a tight ball, then press it to about 1" thick. Space them 2" apart.

### 8) Bake (10–12 min)
- Bake one sheet at a time at **350°F (177°C)**, rotating halfway. Use a cool sheet for each batch.
- Start checking at **10 minutes**. Pull them when the edges are set and lightly golden and the centers still look puffy and slightly underdone. Overbaking dries them out and brings the crumble back.
- If you probe one, try about **190°F** in the center as a starting point. This target hasn't been confirmed on this dough, so note what works.

### 9) Cool on the sheet (15 min)
Leave them on the sheet for **about 15 minutes**, until they're firm enough to lift, then move them to a rack. They're fragile while hot and will flatten a little as they cool.

## Test-batch tasting checklist
Change one thing at a time, so you can tell what fixed it.

- **Still crumbly** → first confirm you did the full rest and pulled them while the centers looked underdone. Next batch, grind more of the oats (60 g ground + 80 g whole). If that's not enough, add ½ tsp psyllium husk powder to the dry mix, the same binder used in the [GF breakfast cookies](breakfast-cookies-gf.md).
- **Still too heavy** → cut the sunflower butter to 100 g. If the dough gets stiff, add milk or water (1–2 tsp).
- **Too little sunflower flavor** → raise the sunflower butter to 130 g.
- **Cakey or too puffy** → cut the baking powder to ¼ tsp, and check that you stopped beating at smooth in step 3.
- **Too thick, didn't spread** → press the mounds to ¾" next time.
- **Spread too flat** → chill the shaped mounds for 20 minutes before baking.
- **Hard or dry once cool** → pull them 1 minute earlier. If that doesn't fix it, cut the ground oats to 30 g and raise the whole oats to 110 g.
- **Greasy** → the jar may not have been stirred enough. Stir it thoroughly before weighing, since the first scoops from an unstirred jar are mostly oil.
- **Green inside** → this is harmless, and the taste and texture don't change. It's the baking soda reacting with the sunflower butter, and it can show up after cooling. To reduce it, try ⅛ tsp baking soda and ¾ tsp baking powder.
- **Good: chewy, holds together, lighter sunflower taste** → write down the bake time, center temp, and cookie weight, then update this recipe out of v1.

## Scaling & make-ahead
- **Double batch (~34 cookies):** same method, same 30-minute rest. Bake one sheet at a time so the oven temperature stays steady.
  - 200 g whole oats (2½ cups) + 80 g oats for grinding (1 cup)
  - 1 tsp baking powder
  - ½ tsp baking soda
  - ¾ tsp fine sea salt (½ tsp if your sunflower butter contains salt)
  - 56 g butter (¼ cup)
  - 230 g sunflower butter (about 14 Tbsp)
  - 170 g brown sugar (about ¾ cup, packed)
  - 42 g honey (2 Tbsp)
  - 2 large eggs (about 100 g) + 2 egg yolks (about 36 g)
  - 1 Tbsp vanilla extract
  - 140 g mini candy-coated chocolates
  - 110 g mini chocolate chips
- **Cold ingredients at double batch:** follow [If ingredients can't come to room temp](#if-ingredients-cant-come-to-room-temp) with these amounts: eggs (2 large, about 100 g, + 2 yolks, about 36 g), butter (56 g / ¼ cup) or neutral oil (42 g / 3 Tbsp), and sunflower butter (230 g / about 14 Tbsp). Warm 4 whole eggs, since 2 are separated for their yolks.
- **Freezing dough:** freeze the shaped mounds on a sheet until solid, then bag them. Bake from frozen at 350°F, adding 2–3 minutes.
- **Storing baked cookies:** keep them airtight at room temp for 3–4 days, or freeze for up to 2 months.
